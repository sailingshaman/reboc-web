1. Purpose

This document describes the systematic process by which Reboc B.V. identifies, assesses, prevents and mitigates potentially negative impacts of its operations and business relationships on human rights. This risk analysis procedure is aligned with the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, the OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises, and applicable Dutch and EU legislation including the Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD).

2. Scope

Reboc B.V. delivers IT services to its clients through independent specialists, each working through their own company. Reboc does not employ those specialists and does not supply personnel to work under the direction and supervision of a third party. The risk analysis covers all aspects of Reboc's business activities, including:

  • Direct operations: Reboc's own personnel, office environment and business conduct
  • The independent companies Reboc contracts to deliver services at client sites
  • Client relationships, and the working environment in which the agreed services are delivered
  • Subcontractors and other business partners in the service delivery chain

3. Risk analysis process

The risk analysis follows a structured five-step cycle that is repeated at least annually and whenever a material change occurs in operations, business relationships or the regulatory environment.

Step 1: Identification of risk areas

We map our business activities and relationships to identify areas where human rights risks could arise. For Reboc B.V. as a company delivering IT services through independent specialists, the primary risk areas are:

  • Contract terms and fair dealing: the terms, rates and payment conditions agreed with the independent companies Reboc contracts
  • Non-discrimination: equal treatment in engagement and in the continuation of assignments, regardless of protected characteristics
  • Health and safety: the conditions at client premises in which services are delivered, including ergonomics and psychosocial risks for remote and on-site work
  • Data privacy: handling of personal data of specialists, candidates and client contacts
  • Forced labour indicators: ensuring every engagement is entered into voluntarily, with fair payment terms and no undue restrictions
  • Supply chain practices: the human rights practices of the clients and subcontractors we work with

Step 2: Risk assessment and prioritisation

Each identified risk is assessed on two dimensions: the severity of the potential impact (scale, scope and irremediability) and the likelihood of occurrence. Risks are categorised as follows:

HighSevere or irreversibleProbableImmediate corrective action
MediumSignificantPossibleMitigation plan within 30 days
LowMinor or reversibleUnlikelyMonitor and review at next cycle

Step 3: Prevention and mitigation measures

Based on the risk assessment, the following standing measures are in place:

  • Contractual safeguards: agreements with specialists and with clients include clauses requiring compliance with applicable law, non-discrimination standards and the Reboc Human Rights Policy.
  • Onboarding screening: new business relationships are evaluated for human rights risks before engagement. For clients, this includes assessing the environment in which the services will be delivered.
  • Engagement monitoring: regular check-ins with the specialists delivering our services, to verify that agreed conditions are upheld and that the engagement runs as contracted.
  • Grievance mechanism: an accessible reporting channel (info@reboc.nl, telephone, post) for any human rights concern, with a commitment to confidential handling and non-retaliation.
  • Data protection: GDPR-compliant data processing, with documented practices as set out in the Reboc Privacy Notice.

Step 4: Monitoring and tracking

The effectiveness of prevention and mitigation measures is monitored through:

  • Periodic check-ins with specialists during their assignments, at least quarterly
  • Review of any grievances or concerns received, and the actions taken
  • Tracking of contract compliance, including timely payment for accepted work
  • Annual review of client and supplier relationships against the Human Rights Policy
  • Monitoring of relevant legal and regulatory developments

Step 5: Reporting and continuous improvement

Findings from the risk analysis are documented and reviewed by the management of Reboc B.V. at least annually. The review evaluates whether existing measures are effective, whether new risks have emerged, and what improvements are needed. Key outcomes include:

  • An updated risk register reflecting current risk levels and any changes
  • Corrective action plans for any identified issues, with clear ownership and deadlines
  • Adjustments to the Human Rights Policy or operational procedures where necessary
  • Communication of material changes to relevant stakeholders

4. Governance and accountability

Ultimate responsibility for this risk analysis process rests with the management of Reboc B.V. As a small organisation, the director is directly involved in all steps of the process and personally oversees key business relationships. This ensures that human rights considerations are embedded in day-to-day decision-making rather than delegated to a separate compliance function.

5. Record keeping

All risk analysis documentation, including risk registers, assessment outcomes, corrective action plans and review notes, is retained for a minimum of five years. Records are stored securely and made available to clients, auditors or regulators upon legitimate request.
